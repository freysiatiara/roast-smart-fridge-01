# Candidate panel — Roast the Smart Fridge (one line, max 160 chars)

Judge criteria, in order: (1) roasts a smart home gadget the writer lives with, in
one line; (2) genuinely funny — a real laugh with a turn or sting at the end, not a
mild observation; (3) fresh — original angle/image/phrasing, never a stock joke
(the brief explicitly says a stock joke about a fridge ordering too much milk
loses); (4) sets up fast, lands inside the limit, no padding, no second sentence;
(5) specific to the gadget — names a real behaviour of the thing.

## Candidates (all my own wording, all single lines)

| # | Chars | Gadget | Line |
|---|-------|--------|------|
| A | 130 | smart fridge | My smart fridge knows exactly what I eat, when I eat it, and still acts surprised every time I open it at 2 a.m. looking for cake. |
| B | 133 | robot vacuum | My robot vacuum has mapped my whole house, memorised the dog's favourite spot, and still gets stuck on the same rug every single day. |
| C | 107 | smart speaker | My smart speaker heard me say 'I'm broke' once, and now it only ever replies with things I can buy on sale. |
| D | 118 | doorbell camera | My doorbell camera has seen me in my pyjamas more often than my own mother has — and unlike her, it keeps the footage. |
| E | 136 | smart thermostat | My smart thermostat lets me set any temperature I want, then changes it back the moment I leave the room, like a landlord with a remote. |
| F | 116 | smart fridge | My smart fridge dims its light when I open it at midnight, as if the leftovers needed a mood and I needed a witness. |

## Head-to-head (against the 4 judge criteria)

- A vs B: both specific and two-beat, but B's kicker ("stuck on the same rug")
  is a familiar robot-vacuum gag; A's "acts surprised" personification is fresher.
  A beats B.
- A vs C: C's turn is sharp — a listener that only monetises you — and it lands
  in 107 chars with zero padding. C's mechanism (surveillance → sales) is more
  original than A's (fridge knows my habits). C beats A.
- C vs D: D has two beats where C has one. D sets up with a relatable, slightly
  embarrassing comparison ("more often than my own mother") and then reframes it
  with a darker kicker ("it keeps the footage") — the gadget as a permanent
  witness, not just a listener. D is also the most repeatable line of the panel:
  the kind of thing you would actually say to someone else. D beats C.
- D vs E: E is clever but its second metaphor ("landlord with a remote") dilutes
  the sting and spends 136 chars; D lands harder in 118. D beats E.
- D vs F: F is poetic but its laugh is soft — a mood, not a sting. D's kicker
  ("keeps the footage") is a concrete, slightly menacing fact about a real
  doorbell camera. D beats F.

## Winner: D (118 chars)

"My doorbell camera has seen me in my pyjamas more often than my own mother has
— and unlike her, it keeps the footage."

Why it wins: gadget named and behaviour real (doorbell cameras record and store
visitors); two beats (setup: mother comparison → reframe: the footage is kept);
the sting lands at the very end; no stock joke anywhere near it; nothing wasted.
